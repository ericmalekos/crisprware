Description

This track shows the DNA sequences targetable by CRISPR RNA guides using the AsCas12a (Cpf1) enzyme from Acidaminococcus sp. BV3L6 (PAM: TTTV) over the entire $organism ($db) genome. CRISPR target sites were annotated with predicted specificity (off-target effects) and predicted efficiency (on-target cleavage) by the tool crisprware, using its built-in crispr-ots off-target engine.

Cas12a differs from Cas9 in three ways that shape this track. Its PAM lies 5' of the protospacer rather than 3', so the thin bar is drawn to the left of the thick bar on the + strand. Its guide is 23 nt rather than 20 nt. And it cuts distal to the PAM, producing a staggered double-strand break with a 5-nt 5' overhang rather than a blunt cut next to the PAM (Zetsche et al. 2015).

This track is specific to AsCas12a (wild type, as used in 2xNLS constructs), which cleaves TTTV but not TTTT PAMs. Both the guides shown and the off-targets counted against them are therefore restricted to TTTV. The engineered broad-PAM variant enCas12a does cleave TTTT and has a much wider PAM range; it is a separate track and the numbers here should not be read as applying to it.

Display Conventions and Configuration

The track shows all potential TTTV target sites across the genome. The 23-nt protospacer (the part matching the guide RNA) is shown with a thick (exon) bar. The 4-nt PAM motif match (TTTV) is shown with a thinner bar, 5' of the protospacer. Guides are colored to reflect both predicted specificity and efficiency. Specificity reflects the "uniqueness" of the 23mer sequence in the genome; the less unique a sequence is, the more likely it is to cleave other locations of the genome (off-target effects). Efficiency is the frequency of cleavage at the target site (on-target efficiency).

Shades of gray stand for sites that are hard to target specifically, as the 23mer is not very unique in the genome:

impossible to target: target site has at least one identical copy in the genome and was not scored (unique = False)
hard to target: the site has at least one off-target at 1 or 2 mismatches
hard to target: aligned, but a marginal AsCas12a specificity of 0.20 to 0.50 (see below)
hard to target: aligned, but a low AsCas12a specificity below 0.20

Colors highlight targets that are specific in the genome (AsCas12a specificity > 0.50, no close off-targets) but have different predicted efficiencies:

low predicted cleavage: DeepCpf1 efficiency percentile <= 30
medium predicted cleavage: DeepCpf1 efficiency percentile > 30 and <= 55
high predicted cleavage: DeepCpf1 efficiency percentile > 55

The Color by: dropdown in the track controls switches between three pre-computed color schemes for the same guides:

  1. Efficiency (DeepCpf1), the default: the blended scheme in the two tables above. Gray for guides that are hard to target, otherwise the red / yellow / green efficiency ramp on the DeepCpf1 (Kim 2018) percentile.
  2. Efficiency (seq-DeepCpf1 AsCas12a): the same scheme, ramped instead on the AsCas12a-specific seq-DeepCpf1 variant model (Chen 2025). The two models disagree on a minority of guides, and this view makes that disagreement visible.
  3. Off-target specificity (AsCas12a): specificity alone, with no gray tier and no efficiency contribution. Red at specificity <= 0.50, olive from 0.50 to 0.70, green above 0.70. Because this scheme never grays a guide out, it keeps reporting specificity for guides that the default scheme has already grayed.

Mouse-over a target site to show predicted specificity and efficiency scores:

  1. The AsCas12a specificity score summarizes all off-targets into a single number. The higher the number, the fewer off-target effects are expected. It is a CFD-style aggregate in the GuideScan2 form, 1 / sum(CFD over the on-target and all off-targets), so a guide with no detected off-target scores 1.0 and a guide sharing the genome with many close matches approaches 0. Guides above 0.50 are colored by efficiency rather than grayed.
  2. The DeepCpf1 efficiency score (Kim et al. 2018) predicts whether a guide leads to rather strong or weak cleavage. Scores are given as percentiles, e.g. "70%" means that 70% of the guides in this genome have a score equal or lower than this guide. The raw score is also shown on the details page.
  3. The seq-DeepCpf1 AsCas12a variant score (Chen et al. 2025) is a second, more recent on-target model trained per Cas12a variant. It is reported for the same guide so the two predictions can be compared; neither is treated as authoritative here.

Click onto features to show all scores and predicted off-targets with up to four mismatches.

Off-target sites are sorted by the CFD (Cutting Frequency Determination) score. The higher the CFD score, the more likely there is off-target cleavage at that site. Off-targets with a CFD score < 0.023 are not shown on this page: when the equivalent Cas9 threshold was compared against experimentally validated off-targets by Haeussler et al. 2016, the large majority of predicted off-targets below it were false positives. For storage and performance reasons, on the level of individual off-targets, only CFD scores are available.

Each listed off-target carries its own CFD score, and the histogram above the list counts the same off-targets the list shows, binned by mismatch count. Guides with more than 1000 off-targets above the CFD threshold are not listed individually: the histogram still reports the true counts, but the list itself is omitted, since a truncated list would misrepresent where the risk lies.

Methods

Relationship between predictions and experimental data

Both axes of this track are predictions, and neither is a precise one. For off-targets, the CFD-style aggregate inherits the limits of the mismatch-activity matrix it is built from: it is derived from measured activity at single mismatches, and it assumes the penalties of multiple mismatches multiply. Real off-target cleavage is also shaped by chromatin, by guide expression level and by the duration of nuclease exposure, none of which the score sees. A high specificity score therefore means "few sequences in this genome resemble this guide", not "this guide was shown to be clean".

For on-target efficiency, the two models shown here were trained on different library screens, and they disagree on a substantial minority of guides. That disagreement is informative and is the reason both are shipped, and the reason the second model gets its own coloring rather than being hidden behind the first. As a general rule the higher the score, the less likely that a guide is very inefficient, but a low-scoring guide can still cut well.

Efficiency percentiles are ranked over every guide in this genome, not against a fixed cross-species reference panel. So a percentile answers "how does this guide compare to the alternatives in this genome", and percentiles are directly comparable between any two guides in the track, on any chromosome. The raw scores are also reported, and are what to use when comparing guides across genomes.

Where the two kinds of evidence conflict, specificity is the one that is weighted: an inefficient guide wastes an experiment, whereas a non-specific guide can produce a result that looks real. This is why the default coloring lets gray override the efficiency ramp, and why the score column and the browser score filter are driven by specificity rather than by efficiency.

Track methods

The entire $organism ($db) genome was scanned for the TTTV motif, and the flanking 23mer protospacer sequences were enumerated. Each guide was scored for off-targets with crispr-ots, the Rust off-target engine bundled with crisprware, against a TTTV index of the same genome, allowing up to 4 mismatches and no bulges. Mismatches are counted over the full 23-nt protospacer. Each off-target was assigned a CFD-style score from the 2xNLS-Cas12a mismatch-activity matrix of DeWeirdt et al. 2021, which gives a per-position, per-base-pair penalty from measured activity; the per-off-target score is the product of the penalties of its mismatches. Per-guide specificity is 1 / sum(score) over the on-target and all off-targets, the GuideScan2 denominator convention (Schmidt et al. 2025). Guides with a perfect match elsewhere in the genome are reported as not unique, their specificity columns are left blank, and they carry no off-target list.

On-target efficiency was scored with two convolutional models: DeepCpf1 (Kim et al. 2018), the original Cas12a activity CNN, and the AsCas12a-specific seq-DeepCpf1 variant model of Chen et al. 2025. Raw scores and their genome-wide percentiles are both reported. The two models are on different scales, so only their percentiles are directly comparable.

Note that the off-target search is restricted to TTTV PAMs throughout, because AsCas12a does not cleave TTTT. A TTTT-adjacent site that otherwise matches a guide is not an AsCas12a off-target and is deliberately absent from these counts, scores and colors. Tracks for the engineered enCas12a variant, which does cleave TTTT, use a correspondingly broader PAM set and are not interchangeable with this one.

Data Access

Positional data can be explored interactively with the Table Browser or the Data Integrator. For small programmatic positional queries, the track can be accessed using the UCSC REST API. For genome-wide data or automated analysis, the CRISPR genome annotations can be downloaded from the UCSC download server as a bigBed file.

The files for this track are called ascas12a.bb, which lists positions and scores, and ascas12a_crisprDetails.tab.gz, which has information about off-target matches. The column definitions are in ascas12aTargets.as. Individual regions or whole genome annotations can be obtained using the tool bigBedToBed, which can be compiled from the source code or downloaded as a pre-compiled binary for your system. Instructions for downloading source code and binaries can be found here. The tool can also be used to obtain only features within a given range, e.g.

bigBedToBed http://hgdownload.soe.ucsc.edu/gbdb/${db}/${track}/ascas12a.bb -chrom=chr21 -start=0 -end=1000000 stdout
